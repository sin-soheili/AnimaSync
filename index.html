live · loudness drives the mouth

How it goes

  1. Drop in a sprite sheet of mouth positions — quietest frame first, widest last. Any count from two up.
  2. Record straight into the page, or drag in a WAV or MP3 you already have.
  3. Line the frames up, frame the shot, watch the preview.
  4. Export a transparent PNG sequence and drop it on your footage.

Streaming instead of editing? Hit Go live and skip steps 2 and 4 — the mic drives the cat in realtime, in a pop‑out window OBS can capture.

What's in it

Record in the browser

Mic straight into the pipeline. Auto gain, noise suppression and echo cancellation are all switched off — every one of them flattens the loud-against-quiet difference the sync is reading.

Slicing editor

Origin, cell size and gutter, dragged on the sheet itself. Auto‑detect reads the grid off the image and doesn't mistake the gap between a sprite's legs for a cell boundary.

Onion-skin align

Generated sheets never come back pixel‑identical across cells. Ghost or difference blend, arrow keys, and an auto‑align that weights the silhouette over the mouth — the one part allowed to differ.

Live, in its own window

Skip the audio file: point it at your mic and the cat pops out into a window of its own, animating in realtime. Hand that to OBS as a window capture and chroma‑key it. Minimize the bar and the window is nothing but cat.

Exports that aren't broken

PNG sequences in a ZIP written by hand, with exact alpha. WebM too, with the duration patched into the container and encoder lag tracked — otherwise you get a clip that's quietly seconds short.

How the sync works

Amplitude, not phonemes. Loud is open, quiet is closed. Audio is split into one window per video frame, each window's RMS becomes dB, and that's normalized against the 95th percentile of the non‑silent windows rather than the peak — so one loud pop doesn't squash the rest of the take. Hysteresis stops a value sitting on a band edge from chattering, and a minimum hold stops the mouth strobing.

Real phoneme lip sync needs a transcript and a lot more machinery. At the size a character actually sits on screen, this reads fine.
